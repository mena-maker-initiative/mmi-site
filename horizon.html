The Horizon Challenge

Build something that matters. The Horizon Challenge is MMI's flagship 48-hour international virtual engineering innovation challenge, bringing together students from around the world to tackle real-world problems through engineering.

You don't need a perfect prototype. You don't need expensive equipment. You need an idea worth exploring and the determination to build it.

August 14–16, 2026 Solo or Teams of Up to 3 Registration Open Now

Eligibility: open to high school students worldwide, ages 13 and up. International participants welcome — the challenge runs fully virtually.

01FormatHow It Starts

Everyone starts from the same line

At the official start of the challenge, five challenge statements are revealed at once — real problem areas, not fixed briefs. Teams pick one and build their entire submission around it for the next 48 hours. Nobody gets a head start on the actual problem, no matter how early they registered.

02PolicyAI Use

AI is welcome. Engineering judgment comes first.

AI tools are welcome for brainstorming, research, coding support, debugging, prototyping, and design exploration. But every participant must understand, verify, and be able to explain every part of their submission. AI should accelerate your engineering process — it can't replace your engineering thinking.

03RequirementsWhat to Submit

Five parts to every submission

01

Project Information

Project title, team member names, contact email, challenge category.

02

Problem Statement

What real-world problem are you solving, and why does it matter?

03

Your Solution

Your engineering approach, how it works, and the reasoning behind your design decisions.

04

Supporting Materials

Anything that helps judges understand your work:

  • GitHub repository
  • Live website or application
  • Wokwi simulation
  • CAD files / PCB designs
  • Circuit diagrams, images, or screenshots
  • Research documentation or prototype photos
05

Project Presentation

Either a 2–3 minute video, or a 1–2 page PDF summary, covering: what you built, what problem it solves, how it works, what tools you used, what challenges you overcame, and — if you used AI — how it contributed to your process.

04EvaluationJudging Criteria

Independent Judging Panel. Projects are evaluated by an independent panel of university engineering students, researchers, and industry professionals — to ensure a fair and technically rigorous judging process.

Real-World Impact

05RulesGuidelines

Keep it fair, keep it honest

What "48 hours" actually means: during the official challenge period, you're expected to develop the core of your project — the engineering solution itself should be conceived and developed during the challenge, once your team's chosen statement is revealed. Preparing beforehand is fully welcome: forming your team, learning new tools, installing software, and gathering materials are all fair game ahead of time.

  • Open to high school students worldwide, ages 13 and up — international participants welcome, the challenge runs fully virtually.
  • Participants may compete individually or in teams of up to three.
  • Your challenge statement is chosen from the five revealed at the official start — the core engineering solution is built during the 48-hour window, not before.
  • Pre-challenge prep (team formation, learning tools, installing software, gathering materials) is welcome and encouraged.
  • All submissions must be original.
  • Existing libraries, open-source software, and publicly available resources may be used where appropriately credited.
  • AI is permitted under the AI policy above.
  • Participants should be ready to explain every aspect of their submission if a judge asks.
  • Be respectful, collaborative, and uphold academic integrity throughout.
