ISEF & University Playbook

The structural mechanics of getting from a finished blueprint to a real submission — which fairs actually feed into ISEF, how to turn a build into a research abstract, and how to fit all of it around your actual school year.

01ResourceRegional Pipeline Index

Where the ISEF pathway actually exists in your country

Researched directly, not assumed. Each entry below is tagged by how confident we are — confirmed pathways we found current evidence for, ones that need a direct check because our sources were dated, and real gaps where we found nothing. Society for Science's own Find-a-Fair directory is always the final word.

Egypt Confirmed

ISEF Egypt (run by the Arab Science & Technology Foundation) is the official Society-affiliated pathway. EISTF is a second, separate Cairo-based fair also open nationwide — worth entering both.

Saudi Arabia Confirmed

The Mawhiba Science & Engineering Fair, run by the King Abdulaziz & His Companions Foundation for Giftedness and Creativity, sent finalists to Regeneron ISEF 2026 — an active, current pathway.

Jordan Confirmed

The Science Fair of the Jordanian Ministry of Education sent finalists to Regeneron ISEF 2026. A separate national fair supported by the Abdul Hameed Shoman Foundation has also historically fed into ISEF.

United Arab Emirates Verify Directly

The Think Science program (Emirates Foundation) has historically been a pathway to ISEF, but our most current confirmation of it is several years old. Dubai also runs its own science fair (DSEF) — affiliation status unconfirmed. Check Find-a-Fair before relying on either.

Bahrain No Fair Found

We could not confirm a Society-affiliated fair based in Bahrain. If you're based there, go directly to Find-a-Fair on societyforscience.org — affiliations open and change, and this is a real gap in the regional pipeline.

Kuwait, Lebanon, Morocco, Tunisia, Oman, Qatar Not Yet Researched

We did not find current, reliable evidence of active pathways for these countries in this pass. That doesn't mean nothing exists — it means check Find-a-Fair directly rather than trust an absence on this list.

This index will grow as MMI's Academic & Strategy Coordinator role gets filled — see the Board section. For now, treat every entry here as a lead to verify, not a final answer, and always cross-check against Society for Science's official directory before building a timeline around it.
02GuideThe Project Blueprint

Turning a hardware build into an ISEF-compliant submission

A finished blueprint from the Vault is a working build, not yet a research project. Here's exactly what ISEF requires, sourced from the official rules — and the actual steps to get from "I built a sensor array" to a submission that meets every one of them.

250
Word Abstract Limit

One page, on the official ISEF abstract form, describing only the student's own work.

240×122×76
Max Board Size (cm)

Height × width × depth — confirm against your specific affiliated fair, as some vary slightly from the ISEF standard.

12
Months Max Research

Continuous research window — can't combine work from multiple unrelated years into one submission.

Also required: Checklist Form 1, Student Checklist Form 1A, and the Research Plan & Approval Form 1B for every project — plus Form 1C if any work happened somewhere other than home, school, or field. Every graphic on your board (photos, charts, clip art) needs individual citation — a blanket "all graphics by student" statement is no longer accepted. All materials must be in English.

From finished build to research submission

01

Find the real question inside the build

A blueprint proves a technique works. A research project asks something the technique can answer. The question usually appears the moment you ask "what could I measure with this, that I don't already know the answer to?"

BP-001's sensor array → "Does greenhouse humidity variance predict next-day plant stress better than temperature alone?"
02

Define your variables before you build further

What are you changing (independent variable), what are you measuring (dependent variable), and what are you holding constant (controls)? Write these down before collecting data — judges specifically look for this.

03

Collect data over real time, not one afternoon

This is exactly what BP-002's SD logging and RTC timestamps are for — a single reading is an anecdote, a logged dataset over days or weeks is evidence.

04

Write the abstract last, not first

Problem statement (1–2 sentences), methodology (2–3 sentences), key results (2–3 sentences), conclusion (1–2 sentences). All of it under 250 words, describing what you actually found — not what you hoped to find.

05

Design the board around your data, not your build

Judges spend more time on your results section than your hardware photos. Lead with the graph or finding that answers your question; the build itself is supporting evidence, not the headline.

03GuideStandardized Integration Timeline

Fitting research around exams, not instead of them

A template to adapt, not a fixed calendar — every school's exam schedule differs. The point is the shape: start early, log data continuously in the background, and never let a research deadline collide with your actual exams.

Term 1

Build & question phase

  • Work through a Vault blueprint end to end — this is where your technical foundation comes from
  • Identify your real research question using the flow in Section 02 above
  • Check the Regional Pipeline Index for your country's fair registration windows — most open in autumn
Term 2

Data collection — kept deliberately lightweight

  • Let a logging build (like BP-002) run in the background collecting data — this should cost you minutes a week, not hours
  • Do not schedule new research work during exam weeks — a data logger running unattended is fine; starting new experiments is not
  • Draft your abstract early, even roughly — it's far easier to tighten a bad draft than write one from nothing under deadline pressure
Term 3 / Spring

Fair season

  • Local and national affiliated fairs generally run January–April — confirm your specific fair's date early, don't assume
  • Finalize your board and abstract at least two weeks before your fair, not the night before
  • If you advance, Regeneron ISEF itself is typically in May — hold that week deliberately open

The one rule that matters most: if a research deadline and an exam ever genuinely conflict, the exam wins. A missed fair registration is recoverable next cycle. A damaged transcript is not. Build the research around your academic calendar — never the other way around.
