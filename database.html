Opportunity Database

Competitions, Olympiads, scholarships, universities, research programs, fellowships, internships, grants, conferences, summer schools, and exchange opportunities — one searchable place, tailored to where you're actually starting from. Read the note below first, though — it matters more than any of it.

Important, before anything else on this page: the trophy isn't the point. ISEF, the programs below, MMI's own contests — every one of them exists to give you a reason to keep building and a room to show it in. They are not the goal. The actual value was always the project itself — the thing you got curious enough to finish, the bug you chased down at 2am, the question you followed because it mattered to you, not because a judge might like it.

Competitions are one of the places you can bring that work once it exists. They are not why you should start. Build things that matter to you first — this page just makes sure that when you're ready to show someone, you know exactly where to go, with a clear guideline and real resources built for where you're starting from, not generic advice written for someone else's context.

01ExplainerWhat ISEF Actually Is

The world's largest pre-college science fair — and how you actually get there

Regeneron ISEF (International Science and Engineering Fair) is a week-long international competition where around 1,800 finalists from more than 75 countries present original research for judging, scholarships, and prizes worth several million dollars combined.

The important part almost nobody explains clearly: you can't apply to ISEF directly. You qualify by placing highly at a "Society-affiliated" fair in your own country or region first — ISEF is the last stop in a chain, not something you enter cold. Fair season generally runs from around January through April, with the international event itself in May.

Every entry is one project, individual or in a team of up to three, built on a maximum of twelve continuous months of research.

Egypt

Two active pathways

ISEF Egypt, run by the Arab Science & Technology Foundation (ASTF), is the official Society-for-Science affiliate — the direct route to Regeneron ISEF. EISTF (Egypt International Science & Technology Fair) is a second, separate Cairo-based fair also open to students nationwide. Worth entering both if eligible.

Bahrain — Known Gap

No dedicated affiliated fair found

We couldn't confirm a Society-affiliated fair based in Bahrain at the time of writing. If you're based there, check Society for Science's official Find-a-Fair directory directly (societyforscience.org) before assuming there's no path — affiliations open and change. This is a real gap in the regional pipeline, and part of why the Playbook module exists.

Anywhere in MENA

Default: Find-a-Fair

If your country isn't listed above, the Society for Science's official directory is the authoritative source for what's currently affiliated near you — it updates as fairs affiliate each year, so check it directly rather than relying on any static list, including this one.

02CategoryResearch & Summer Programs

Research programs and camps open to students outside the US

Every program below explicitly accepts international students and lists real deadlines we could verify. Dates shift slightly year to year — always confirm the exact date on the program's own site before you plan around it.

Fall Deadlines (Oct–Dec)

Yale Young Global Scholars (YYGS) — Early Action

Two-week residential program at Yale across Science & Technology, Politics & Economics, or Global Challenges tracks. Need-based aid available to international students, up to full tuition.

Early Action ~mid-OctGrades 10–11

Telluride Association Summer Seminars (TASS)

Fully-funded six-week seminar program (travel included) for rising juniors, with a long track record of producing scholars and public leaders.

~Mid-JanuaryFully funded

Yale Young Global Scholars — Regular Decision

Same program as above; later deadline if you miss Early Action.

~Early JanuaryGrades 10–11

Summer Science Program (SSP)

39-day residential research program — teams calculate real asteroid orbits or run genomics/biochemistry research using professional tools. Rising seniors from any country; need-based aid available.

Late FebruaryRising seniors

Garcia Research Scholars (Stony Brook University)

Seven-week lab research program in materials science, biomedical engineering, or molecular biology, working on real faculty-led projects.

Early–mid FebruaryJuniors & seniors

ASSIP (George Mason University)

Eight-week full-time research role under university faculty across robotics, neuroscience, environmental science, and bioengineering. Remote and in-person tracks.

Early FebruaryAge 15+
